# Examples: before and after

This file shows research text before and after the change to Simplified Research English.
The examples are our own examples. The numbers and the citations in the examples are not real.

## Example 1: Descriptive text

Before:

> It has been shown that the proposed controller can be leveraged to significantly improve robustness; however, the gains may potentially be tuned poorly, which would lead to instability, i.e., the state diverges.

After:

> Smith (2020) showed that the proposed controller increases the stability margin of the two-mass system.
> But a poor choice of the gains can make the closed loop unstable.
> As a result, the state diverges.

The change: no perfect tense, no passive voice, no semicolon, and no Latin abbreviation. The text does not use “leverage”, “may”, “would”, or “significantly” without a test. Each sentence has one topic.

## Example 2: A result

Before:

> The proposed approach performs significantly better than the baseline.

After:

> On the 50 test problems, the new solver decreases the mean computation time by 12% relative to the interior-point method (Welch t-test).

The change: the scope, the two sides of the comparison, the metric, the amount, and the statistical test (rules R2, R4, and R5).

## Example 3: Procedure with a caution

Before:

> Before running the experiments, the config file should be updated and the old results should probably be backed up, since they'll be overwritten.

After:

> CAUTION: Make a copy of the results folder before you start. The script overwrites all previous runs.
> 
> 1. Open the configuration file.
> 2. Set the number of runs to 50.
> 3. Run the script.

The change: a caution before the steps, the imperative, one instruction in each step, no “should”, and no contraction.

## Example 4: Vertical list

Before:

> When you share the results, don't upload the raw logs, the API keys, or the survey data.

After:

> When you share the results:
> 
> - Do not upload the raw logs.
> - Do not upload the API keys.
> - Do not upload the survey data.

The change: a vertical list, and “not” in each item.
