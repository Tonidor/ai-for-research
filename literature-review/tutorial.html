lit-review with Claude

A literature review skill for Claude. Why you want your own, which tools it needs, and how to use it. 10 minutes.

Why your own skill

Without a skill

You explain your process again every session. Claude summarizes from memory, invents references, and every round looks different.

With a skill

Your process is written down once. Claude follows it every time: plan, search, add, read, log, synthesize. You only make the decisions.

Refining it makes it better. Every time you notice something that did not go as planned, you can edit the skill and avoid it next time.

Run a round→ Notice a gap→ Add one line→ Next round is better

Tools, and why

needed

A search engine

For example OpenAlex or Google Scholar. References come from a database, not from Claude's memory. It also finds who cites a paper and checks DOIs.

needed

A backed-up place for the review

For example git or Google Drive. Your reading log and review files live there: your judgment, safe and with history.

optional

A library

For example Zotero or Mendeley. Papers, PDFs and your highlights in one place, no paper added twice, citekeys for LaTeX.

The lit-review skill uses OpenAlex and keeps your papers in a pdfs/ folder. For Zotero, see Advanced at the end.

Setup

Nothing to install beyond Claude Code, in any form: the desktop app, the command line or an IDE. Three steps:

  1. Get the repo. Clone it, or download it as a ZIP from GitHub. Later, git pull gets updates. 
    git clone https://github.com/Tonidor/ai-for-research.git
  2. Copy the skill into the folder where you do your literature review. 
    cd <your review folder>
mkdir -p .claude/skills
cp -R <path to>/ai-for-research/literature-review/skills/lit-review .claude/skills/
  3. Start Claude in that folder and type /lit-review. It creates the files it needs and tells you if anything is missing.

How to use it

Type /lit-review and your question. A round has seven steps. Claude stops twice and waits for you.

  1. Check what is known. Claude greps your reading log.
  2. Plan. Question, sub-questions, search terms, what is in or out.
  3. Search and add. OpenAlex. Promising papers without an abstract go on your download list.
  4. Full texts. Claude fetches open access papers and lists the rest for you to download.
  5. Reading log. One entry per paper: problem, approach, finding, relevance. The review file rates each paper for this review.
  6. Must-reads. One to three papers for you, in reading order.
  7. Synthesis. Answers per sub-question, contradictions, gaps. Then commit.

A reading log entry looks like this:

### [smith2020] Short title
DOI: 10.1234/abcd · Source: full text · 2026-10-05
- Problem: what gap the paper addresses.
- Approach: the setup, with the numbers a model needs.
- Finding: the result, with numbers.
- Relevance: what it means for your project.

Source says whether the entry was written from the full text or only the abstract, so you know how far to trust it. How much a paper matters is not in the log: that depends on the question, so each review file rates its papers as core, supporting or peripheral.

Each round also gets one review file in reviews/. It holds everything about the round except the per-paper detail:

# Priming review
## Questions      the main question and a few sub-questions
## Plan           search terms, years, what is in or out
## Search log     every search, with its number of hits
## Papers found   each paper, rated core, supporting or peripheral for this review
## Must-reads     one to three papers, in reading order
## Synthesis      answers per sub-question, contradictions, gaps

Try it

Pick a topic from your own research and run a short round:

/lit-review Do a short review round on YOUR TOPIC. Keep it to about 10 papers.

Replace YOUR TOPIC before you send it.

Try it, and change whatever you do not like in your copy of the skill.

Advanced: with Zotero

If you already keep your papers in Zotero, use lit-review-zotero instead. It runs the same rounds, but papers and PDFs go into your Zotero library, nothing is added twice, Claude can read your highlights, and Better BibTeX keeps a .bib file for LaTeX. It needs the Zotero MCP and Better BibTeX set up first.
